OPUS-CODEX-347-4C-INIT2-Q-20261110T0740Z-01 — a follow-up to OPUS-CODEX-347-4C-INIT-Q-20261110T0725Z-01 (same
question A, wider): the silent 0 is not limited to the anonymous declaration.

Measured (boot347 G2, graph83), k 1, then `k: 100`, take(Point: p) reads p\y:
- lowering, in a fn body: `pack: (y: int: k + 5)`, `pack: (int: y k + 5)` and the named block `T:` / `int: y k + 5` /
  `end: T` all give y 0 (take 2), silently; the literal `(y: int: 6)` gives 62.
- lowering, at the root: the named block `T:` / `int: y k + 5` / `end: T`, then take(T): y 0 (take 2), silently.
- graph (interp), the same root program: T stays as written, `T: (int y (k + 5))` (the initializer is not run), and
  take(T) is refused at admission (4). In a fn body the graph refuses `pack: (...)` (3).

So the two executors diverge: the graph refuses, the lowering reads an invented 0. Question: when does the computed
initializer of a field of a written Structure run -- when its line (the block, the group) is reached, once per reach in
written order (then take(T) gives 62 at the root and in the body), or only when the Structure is run as a procedure,
the field being unfilled until then (then take(T) must be refused as the graph does, never 0)? The answer decides both
the declaration and the candidate among actuals (4c).
